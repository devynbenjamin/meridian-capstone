# Lease-Driven Expansion

## The Idea

Meridian's growth from 6 to 14 stores in 5 years has come largely by
taking over leases in neighborhoods that national chains pulled out of,
on the belief those neighborhoods were underserved.

## Why It's Relevant

This is an opportunistic, availability-driven expansion pattern rather
than a purely demand-driven site-selection process. It's directly
relevant to evaluating the Pasadena decision: is Pasadena being
considered because a lease became available, because of demonstrated
underserved demand, or both? The brief calls Pasadena "the obvious next
step" without fully explaining why — worth probing in the interview.

National real estate data adds a wrinkle: grocery-anchored centers
currently run a low 4.0% vacancy rate (vs. 6.3% for non-anchored
properties) and command a 4.4% rent premium, with investment volume up
42% in 2025. That's a strong market for grocery-anchored space generally
— which raises the question of whether the leases Meridian took over were
genuinely undervalued/available, or whether Meridian is paying
current-market rates for space in a tightening leasing environment.
