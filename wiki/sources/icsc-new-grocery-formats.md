# ICSC: New Grocery Formats, Store Growth, and Investment

Source: https://www.icsc.com/news-and-views/icsc-exchange/new-grocery-formats-store-growth-and-investment-signal-sector-in-motion (ICSC Exchange)
Raw capture: [sources-raw/icsc-new-grocery-formats.md](../sources-raw/icsc-new-grocery-formats.md) (fetched summary, not full original text)

## Key Takeaways

- U.S. grocery market: $912.4B. JLL tracks ~40,000 grocery-anchored
  retail centers nationwide; investment in these centers hit $11B in
  2025, up 42% from 2024 — capital is actively flowing into
  grocery-anchored real estate.
- The sector is bifurcating: shoppers are making "more deliberate
  choices," gravitating either to unbeatable pricing at discounters
  (Aldi, Food 4 Less) or to curated, differentiated experiences at
  specialty retailers (Trader Joe's, Whole Foods) — traditional
  mid-market supermarkets (Kroger, Publix, Safeway) are losing ground in
  between.
- Trader Joe's grew 3% YoY, outperforming the sector by 6 points — cited
  as the standout specialty performer.
- Small-format/micromarket stores (3,000-5,400 sq ft) are an emerging
  format, sometimes paired with added services like EV charging (e.g.,
  Gelson's ReCharge). Flexible/modular layouts (Aldi testing) are
  replacing standardized formats.
- Big-box chains are pushing further into grocery as a
  "lifestyle/experience destination": Target's food & beverage segment
  did $24B+ in fiscal 2025; Walmart is remodeling 650+ stores; Costco is
  targeting 30+ new stores/year.
- A behavioral data point: 29% of surveyed diners cut back on fast-food
  visits while 36% increased grocery visits — suggests some trade-down/
  trade-in behavior toward grocery, including prepared foods.
