# Overview

Meridian Markets is a 14-store specialty grocery chain across LA, Orange,
and Ventura counties (~$78M revenue, ~620 employees), competing against
national chains on prepared foods, local sourcing, and a smaller
footprint. It has grown quickly — six stores to fourteen in five years —
largely by taking over leases in neighborhoods larger chains exited, on
the belief those neighborhoods were underserved. See
[Meridian Markets](entities/meridian-markets.md) and
[Lease-Driven Expansion](concepts/lease-driven-expansion.md).

Growth has been uneven: some stores succeeded quickly, others more
slowly, and expansion decisions have historically been made on instinct
rather than data. The immediate ask is a dashboard on sales performance
by store and category to support a decision on a Pasadena location, which
leadership already considers the likely next step but wants data to back
up. There's also an unexploited asset — a ~40,000-member loyalty program
— that could inform a broader understanding of customers, separate from
the site-selection question.

Nationally, the grocery sector is bifurcating: shoppers are gravitating
either to discounters on price or to curated specialty retailers on
experience, with mid-market chains losing ground in between. Specialty
comparables like Trader Joe's are outperforming the sector, and a
parallel trend toward small-format "micromarkets" (3,000-5,400 sq ft) is
gaining investment. Meridian's positioning — smaller footprint, prepared
foods, local sourcing — looks aligned with where the market is actually
moving, not just a defensible niche. See
[Specialty Grocery Positioning](concepts/specialty-grocery-positioning.md)
and [Small-Format Grocery Trend](concepts/small-format-grocery-trend.md).
This also sharpens an open question: is Meridian's growth so far riding
this genuine demand shift, or has it mostly been availability-driven
(leases becoming open) — see
[Lease-Driven Expansion](concepts/lease-driven-expansion.md)?

The primary stakeholder is [Dana Okafor](entities/dana-okafor.md), VP of
Operations, who wrote the brief and is slow but responsive by email; her
assistant handles scheduling only. She wants a preliminary look ready for
a board meeting in about three weeks, inside an overall ~8-week
engagement.

A firm, non-negotiable constraint governs how this team works with the
data at all: per the brief's terms, loyalty and labor data (and any
excerpts) never go into AI tools, while store-level sales totals and
store attributes are fine. This shapes both the analysis workflow and
this wiki itself — see [Client Brief](sources/client-brief.md) and
`docs/data-handling-checklist.md`.

Open threads worth raising in the stakeholder interview: what specifically
makes Pasadena "the obvious next step," what "improve customer
experience" means in practice, and whether there's already an informal
read on why some past expansions outperformed others.
